01 / Executive synthesis

A system for governed autonomy, not just faster code.

The emerging Agentic PDLC combines goal-directed agents, qualified institutional knowledge, contextual decision rights, layered assurance, and two evidence-driven learning loops. Its purpose is to shorten the path from intent to accepted outcomes without making human accountability ceremonial.

The central design question
What must an accountable human understand to authorize consequential agent work—and which obligations cannot be waived even with informed consent?

What has crystallized

Three boundaries to keep visible

Concept ≠ implementation

A graph-like knowledge model

Richly linked, qualified assertions are favored conceptually. No graph database, schema, query engine, or canonical store has been chosen.

Agreement ≠ authorization

A direction, not ratification

The documents capture user positions, tentative agreements and assistant proposals. They do not establish organizationally binding architecture or policy.

Evidence ≠ aspiration

Validation is still owed

Agent independence, risk classification, attention savings, enterprise scalability, and process gains are hypotheses requiring observed outcomes.

The architectural hypothesis

Synthesis — not an adopted design
Intent and obligations guide autonomous work; knowledge discovery and risk controls shape what may be done; assurance supplies evidence; human authority resolves consequential choices; operational results update both product and process.

Two learning loops, not one

Loop A · Product

Did the change deliver and preserve value?

Feature acceptance, invariants, nonfunctional quality, telemetry, customer outcomes and operational acceptance may evolve over time.

Loop B · PDLC

Did the delivery system work well?

Evaluate escalation quality, attention cost, escaped risk, rework, drift, cost per accepted result and routing quality—without optimizing proxies blindly.

Meta-governance

Who may change the rules?

Agents can propose improvements to their own orchestration and controls, but weakening governing constraints requires independent authority and evidence.
